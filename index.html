How the light works

No model, no black box, no keys — Beacon is a formula you could check by hand.

Your twenty-six answers become a profile across thirteen dimensions. Every city carries researched numbers — Numbeo rents, cost-of-living and safety indexes, Wikipedia sunshine hours, its actual languages. Each dimension’s fit is one minus the distance between you and the city, weighted by how much that dimension tends to matter.

Four dimensions bend to your situation instead: language is a capability (shared tongue counts fully, English-friendliness partly, and your willingness to learn carries the rest); cost is one-way (cheaper than your comfort is never penalized, pricier is only forgiven when it buys the quality you named); safety is a floor you set, softened by the tradeoff you chose; diversity counts exactly as much as you said it does.

The weighted average is raised to a power to spread the field, and that is your percentage — same answers, same result, every time. Jobs, pay bands, and homes are seeded formulas over each city’s industries, cost index, and market rent.
